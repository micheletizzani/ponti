# **White-Sutton Syndrome: Molecular Pathogenesis, Emerging Therapeutics, and Multidisciplinary Rehabilitation Strategies**

White-Sutton syndrome (WHSUS; OMIM \#616364) is an autosomal dominant neurodevelopmental disorder caused by heterozygous, predominantly loss-of-function pathogenic variants in the *POGZ* (*pogo* transposable element derived with zinc finger domain) gene on chromosome 1q21.31. First recognized as a distinct clinical entity in 2016, the syndrome is clinically characterized by intellectual disability ranging from mild to profound, gross and fine motor delays, severe expressive language impairments frequently manifesting as childhood apraxia of speech, distinctive craniofacial dysmorphisms, and prominent neuropsychiatric comorbidities including autism spectrum disorder, severe anxiety, and attention deficits2.  
Advances in functional genomics, cellular modeling, and preclinical neuropharmacology have advanced the field beyond initial variant discovery toward defining the chromatin dynamics governing early corticogenesis and evaluating targeted therapeutic repositioning7. Concurrently, prospective registries and multicenter cohorts have delineated systemic manifestations, revealing elevated prevalences of gastrointestinal dysmotility, cyclic vomiting syndrome, adult obesity, and subclinical electroencephalographic abnormalities10.

## **Molecular Pathogenesis and Cellular Neurobiology**

The *POGZ* gene encodes an essential nuclear chromatin regulator that coordinates embryonic neurogenesis, transcriptional stability, and mitotic cell cycle progression1. Structurally, the full-length human POGZ protein comprises an N-terminal cluster of C2H2 zinc finger domains that mediate sequence-specific DNA binding, a central protein-protein interaction domain containing an HP1-binding motif that interacts with heterochromatin protein 1 (HP1, encoded by *CBX5*), and a C-terminal transposase-derived DDE domain homologous to the *pogo* DNA transposase superfamily1. Through these coordinated functional modules, POGZ coordinates the structural packaging and transcriptional output of the developing mammalian genome1.

### **Chromatin Architecture and Transcriptional Regulation**

Contrary to initial assumptions that POGZ acted primarily at condensed heterochromatin via HP1 recruitment, genome-wide cleavage under targets and release using nuclease (CUT\&RUN) and chromatin profiling have established that POGZ occupancy occurs predominantly at euchromatic loci12. More than 90% of physiological POGZ-binding sites overlap active chromatin marks, specifically histone H3 lysine 27 acetylation (H3K27ac) and accessible chromatin regions, with significant enrichment at active embryonic brain enhancers containing homeobox motifs (such as DLX and LHX)12.  
High-resolution chromatin conformation capture (Micro-C) and precision nuclear run-on sequencing (PRO-seq) demonstrate that POGZ safeguards topological genome organization8. Loss of POGZ disrupts topologically associating domain (TAD) boundaries across neuronal gene clusters, triggering a collapse of nascent RNA synthesis for genes governing synaptogenesis and axon guidance7. POGZ complexes with the H3K9 methyltransferases G9a and GLP (*EHMT1* and *EHMT2*), stabilizing chromatin compaction during neuronal differentiation8. Furthermore, POGZ operates in stoichiometric balance with Activity-Dependent Neuroprotective Protein (ADNP) and HP1γ; alterations in *POGZ* gene dosage disrupt this complex, driving transcriptional dysregulation across prefrontal and cerebellar networks12.

### **Neural Progenitor Dynamics and Network Excitability**

In human embryonic stem cell and induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) models, CRISPR-Cas9-engineered deletion of *POGZ* impairs early neural rosette development14. *POGZ*\-deficient neural stem cells demonstrate reduced mitotic activity, evidenced by diminished Ki-67 and phosphorylated histone H3 (PHH3) expression14. This proliferation deficit selectively depletes the intermediate progenitor pool marked by TBR2 (*EOMES*) and disrupts the production of early-born cortical neurons expressing TBR114.  
These developmental arrests alter post-mitotic neuronal function7. Mature cortical neurons derived from *POGZ*\-mutant backgrounds exhibit reduced neurite extension, altered arborization, and atypical spontaneous electrophysiological firing rates7. In rodent models, heterozygous *Pogz* deficiency induces a cell-autonomous state of elevated cortical glutamatergic excitability, disrupting the local excitatory-to-inhibitory (E/I) balance, triggering pro-inflammatory microglial activation, and producing downstream behavioral deficits in social interaction and sensorimotor gating13.

## **Clinical Phenotypic Spectrum and Systemic Comorbidities**

While initially recognized through broad next-generation sequencing cohorts for undifferentiated intellectual disability, expanded worldwide case series have delineated a recognizable multi-system phenotype1. The condition occurs predominantly due to *de novo* heterozygous mutations, though rare instances of familial transmission from affected parents exhibiting milder cognitive deficits confirm significant intrafamilial variability1.

### **Cognitive, Motor, and Speech-Language Profile**

Developmental delay is identified in over 90% of patients, with intellectual disability spanning from borderline-mild learning difficulties to profound cognitive deficits4. Generalized neonatal hypotonia is frequently the earliest sign that brings infants to medical evaluation, contributing to delayed gross motor milestones; unassisted walking is attained between 12 months and 6 years of age4.  
Expressive speech impairment is a defining characteristic of White-Sutton syndrome and is consistently more pronounced than gross motor delay4. Detailed speech pathology assessments classify the verbal phenotype as Childhood Apraxia of Speech (CAS), characterized by a neurogenic motor speech programming deficit that impairs the sequencing of speech sounds and prosody6. Although single words may emerge at an expected age, progress toward multi-word sentences is delayed into school-age years, with a subset of individuals remaining non-verbal into adulthood4. Receptive language comprehension consistently exceeds expressive capabilities, creating an expressive-receptive discordance that exacerbates frustration and behavioral dysregulation4.

### **Neuropsychiatric Architecture and Sleep Pathology**

The neurobehavioral profile of White-Sutton syndrome is characterized by divergent social presentations10. Individuals are often described by clinicians and caregivers as possessing a friendly, affectionate, and chatty demeanor in one-on-one interactions or familiar settings2. However, this is frequently accompanied by social anxiety, hypersensitivity to external stimuli, and emotional dysregulation in larger group environments10.  
Formal autism spectrum disorder is diagnosed in 29% to 50% of cohorts4. Comorbid attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), impulsivity, severe slowness, oppositional behaviors, and self-injurious episodes are also widely documented3. Sleep disturbances represent another significant clinical burden, affecting substantial proportions of patients10. Molecular studies link POGZ directly to the transcriptional regulation of circadian clock genes, providing a biological basis for frequent nocturnal awakenings and circadian rhythm fragmentation, which may be further compounded by obstructive sleep apnea secondary to craniofacial hypotonia19.

### **Neurological, Sensory, and Somatic Manifestations**

Central nervous system magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) reveals structural anomalies in approximately 61% of individuals4. Recurring findings include deep cerebral white-matter hyperintensities on T2/FLAIR sequences, prominence of cerebrospinal fluid in the posterior fossa, mild cerebellar vermis hypoplasia, and a thin corpus callosum4. Overt clinical epilepsy occurs in approximately 20% of patients, presenting as focal motor, generalized tonic-clonic, or febrile seizures4. Even in the absence of overt seizures, routine and sleep-deprived electroencephalography (EEG) identifies subclinical epileptiform discharges and paroxysms in up to 60% of cases10.  
Sensory manifestations are widespread; refractive errors (hyperopia, myopia, astigmatism) and strabismus are detected in roughly half of all cases, while rarer structural ocular anomalies include optic nerve hypoplasia, Horner syndrome, and rod-cone retinal dystrophy1. Sensorineural or mixed hearing loss is documented in 31% of cohorts1.  
Somatic and gastrointestinal complications have emerged as major components of the clinical phenotype10. Chronic gastrointestinal dysfunction affects up to 89% of patients11. A nationwide participatory study confirmed that cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS) fulfilling Rome IV diagnostic criteria affects 9% to 37.5% of pediatric cases, producing episodes of intractable nausea and vomiting that frequently require emergency hospitalization for parenteral hydration10. Severe functional constipation, gastroesophageal reflux disease, and oropharyngeal dysphagia with aspiration risk are also common4. Longitudinal registries have identified a notable susceptibility to overweight and obesity, which is present in up to 76% of adolescent and adult cohorts and warrants long-term metabolic monitoring1.

## **Translational Breakthroughs and Targeted Pharmacotherapies**

There are currently no approved disease-modifying therapies for White-Sutton syndrome, but translational studies in cellular and rodent models have identified targetable biological mechanisms26. Therapeutic discovery now spans small-molecule repurposing, receptor-targeted neuropeptide modulation, and preclinical gene addition strategies27.

### **Perampanel and AMPA Receptor Antagonism**

The most clinically advanced drug repurposing initiative in White-Sutton syndrome targets glutamatergic hyperactivation15. Matsumura and colleagues demonstrated that *Pogz*\-deficient cortical pyramidal neurons exhibit elevated excitatory postsynaptic currents and increased intrinsic excitability15. Pharmacological dampening of this elevated activity using low-dose perampanel (Fycompa)—a non-competitive AMPA glutamate receptor antagonist approved for epilepsy—normalized mature cortical network firing and reversed social interaction deficits in adult mutant mice4.  
These findings led to the launch of a multicenter, interventional, drug-repositioning clinical trial employing a self-controlled case series (SCCS) design across European medical institutions (EudraCT: 2021-001839-77; CTIS EUCT: 2025-520611-14-00)28. The trial evaluates the safety and efficacy of daily perampanel administration over 6 to 18 months in pediatric and adult cohorts with genetically confirmed *POGZ* pathogenic variants28. Therapeutic efficacy is tracked using the Child Behavior Checklist (CBCL) and the Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales (VABS)28. Preliminary results presented by Facchini et al. at the European Society of Human Genetics (ESHG) meeting demonstrated positive shifts in adaptive behavioral scores and quality-of-life indices, providing initial clinical support for AMPA-receptor antagonism in this disorder9.

### **Cannabinoid Modulation in Drug-Resistant Epilepsy**

Although seizures in many patients respond to standard first-line anti-seizure medications, a subset develops drug-resistant developmental and epileptic encephalopathies32. Perilli and colleagues reported the longitudinal efficacy of adjunctive off-label cannabidiol (CBD) in a patient with White-Sutton syndrome who suffered from medically refractory focal motor seizures32. Initiation of purified oral CBD at 10 mg/kg/day achieved complete clinical seizure remission and normalized EEG background patterns32. Parental discontinuation of the treatment against medical advice prompted an immediate relapse of motor seizures, which resolved upon re-initiation of CBD therapy32. This demonstrates the potential of cannabinoid therapeutics to stabilize refractory neurodevelopmental hyperexcitability in *POGZ*\-related epilepsy32.

### **The Oxytocin Receptor Signaling Pathway**

Molecular characterization has identified the oxytocin system as a key downstream transcriptional target of POGZ29. Chromatin immunoprecipitation assays reveal that wild-type POGZ directly binds the core promoter region of the oxytocin receptor gene (*OXTR*); pathogenic *POGZ* mutations disrupt this binding, causing significant downregulation of OXTR mRNA and protein expression in the hippocampus and prefrontal cortex29. In preclinical experiments, administration of intranasal oxytocin (200 ![][image1]g/kg) rescued social memory deficits and normalized social investigation behaviors in *Pogz*\-mutant rodents29. These results suggest that bypassing transcriptional silencing of *OXTR* through receptor agonism may serve as a pharmacological strategy for social communication deficits29.

### **Gene Replacement and High-Throughput Screening**

Translational research programs supported by the Cure POGZ Disorders Foundation and the White Sutton Syndrome Foundation are developing strategies to target the root genetic etiology27. Preclinical roadmaps focus on adeno-associated virus (AAV9)-mediated gene replacement to deliver functional *POGZ* cDNA to the central nervous system27. A primary challenge involves vector packaging constraints and the requirement for precise dosage titration, as non-physiological overexpression of chromatin regulators can disrupt native epigenetic landscapes27.  
In parallel, high-throughput phenotypic drug screens utilizing patient-derived iPSC neurons—spearheaded by academic laboratories including the Hester Lab at Nationwide Children’s Hospital and the Shifman Lab at Hebrew University—are testing compound libraries to identify molecules that restore normal chromatin marks, downregulate neuroinflammatory gene networks, and normalize synaptic firing13.

## **Multidisciplinary Rehabilitation and Clinical Management**

In the absence of approved disease-modifying therapies, management relies on proactive, individualized, multidisciplinary rehabilitation initiated in early infancy to optimize neurodevelopmental potential and mitigate secondary complications4.

### **Speech-Language Pathology and Alternative Communication**

Given the disproportionate severity of expressive language delay relative to receptive comprehension, early and continuous speech-language pathology intervention is essential4. Because the primary speech pathology in White-Sutton syndrome is Childhood Apraxia of Speech, therapeutic frameworks must emphasize motor speech programming rather than solely phonological drill work6. Clinicians utilize motor-learning approaches that incorporate tactile, kinesthetic, and visual cues (such as PROMPT-based principles) alongside high-frequency practice of syllable sequencing and prosodic control6.  
Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) systems should be introduced during toddlerhood rather than withheld as an intervention of last resort4. The introduction of low-tech communication books or high-tech dynamic speech-generating devices with eye-gaze or touch-screen interfaces bridges the gap between receptive understanding and expressive motor execution, reducing severe communicative frustration, tantrums, and secondary self-injurious behaviors4. Routine early videofluoroscopic swallowing studies (VFSS) or fiberoptic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing (FEES) are warranted in infants exhibiting hypotonia or recurrent respiratory infections to detect silent aspiration and guide dietary viscosity modifications4.

### **Physical, Occupational, and Sensory Integration Therapies**

Physical therapy should begin upon detection of neonatal hypotonia to facilitate gross motor milestones4. Interventions focus on core trunk stabilization, pelvic control, and vestibular integration to support transitions toward sitting, quadruped crawling, and independent walking4. Because generalized ligamentous laxity and ankle pronation are frequent, dynamic supramalleolar orthoses (SMOs) or ankle-foot orthoses (AFOs) are often prescribed to provide biomechanical stability during ambulation10.  
Occupational therapy targets fine motor control, bilateral coordination, and adaptive self-care skills, including unassisted feeding and dressing4. Sensory integration protocols are critical to address the sensory processing abnormalities common in this population4. Tailored sensory diets and systematic desensitization protocols help mitigate extreme auditory, tactile, or visual defensiveness, enabling individuals to better tolerate stimulating classroom and public settings4.

### **Neuropsychiatric, Behavioral, and Sleep Interventions**

Managing the neurobehavioral profile requires structured behavioral interventions and environmental predictability4. Structured methodologies, such as the TEACCH framework and visual schedules, help manage executive dysfunction and ease transitions, minimizing the panic or behavioral outbursts triggered by unexpected changes4. Functional behavioral assessments, coupled with Applied Behavior Analysis (ABA) or positive behavioral support programs, are indicated to de-escalate aggressive outbursts, manage disruptive behaviors, and address specific phobias4.  
Comorbid ADHD symptoms often respond to pharmacological intervention, including methylphenidate preparations or alpha-2 adrenergic agonists (guanfacine, clonidine), though doses must be carefully titrated to prevent worsening baseline anxiety4. Addressing sleep fragmentation requires structured sleep hygiene regimens, behavioral bedtime routines, and screening via polysomnography to rule out obstructive sleep apnea4. Supplemental melatonin may assist in entraining disrupted circadian rhythms21.

### **Management of Gastrointestinal Comorbidities**

Given that up to 89% of patients experience gastrointestinal symptoms, clinical teams must establish proactive medical protocols11. For cyclic vomiting syndrome, care pathways should include both prophylactic and abortive components11. Prophylactic management often involves off-label cyproheptadine in young children, or amitriptyline and topiramate in older children11. Abortive protocols require emergency room care plans specifying prompt intravenous hydration with dextrose-containing fluids, electrolyte repletion, and parenteral 5-HT3 receptor antagonists (ondansetron), administered in quiet, low-stimulation environments11.  
Functional constipation must be treated aggressively with daily osmotic laxatives, such as polyethylene glycol, and dietary modifications to prevent impaction, megacolon, and secondary abdominal pain4. Long-term nutritional guidance is necessary from early childhood to prevent the development of adolescent- and adult-onset obesity, with regular metabolic monitoring of lipid panels and glycemic indices1.

## **Clinical Registries, Observational Studies, and Research Infrastructure**

Advancing therapeutic translation in rare genetic conditions depends on natural history data and clinical trial readiness40. Several coordinated clinical and observational platforms are currently active:

### **Prospective Psychiatric Phenotyping (NCT07380594)**

The Centre Hospitalier Universitaire (CHU) Dijon Bourgogne is conducting an observational study entitled *“Descriptive Study of Psychiatric Symptoms in White-Sutton Syndrome”* (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT07380594)41. This protocol systematically characterizes the neuropsychiatric natural history of WHSUS across pediatric, adolescent, and adult cohorts41. By evaluating psychiatric comorbidities, executive functioning, adaptive behaviors, and mood disorders using standardized rating scales, this study aims to define validated clinical endpoints for future interventional drug trials41.

### **Participatory Gastrointestinal Cohorts**

The Association White-Sutton France (AWSF) conducted a national participatory cohort study that mapped the prevalence and features of chronic digestive pathology in WHSUS11. This work formally established the association between *POGZ* mutations and cyclic vomiting syndrome under Rome IV criteria, demonstrating the utility of patient-organization registries in refining the recognized phenotypic spectrum of rare diseases11.

### **Simons Searchlight and Global Registries**

Simons Searchlight maintains an international registry and biorepository for *POGZ*\-related disorders, having enrolled and tracked more than 160 molecularly confirmed individuals40. The registry prospectively aggregates longitudinal clinical histories, standardized neurobehavioral surveys, and diagnostic evaluations40. Furthermore, Simons Searchlight coordinates the collection of biospecimens—including peripheral blood mononuclear cells, genomic DNA, and derived iPSC lines—distributing these standardized resources to the academic research community to facilitate disease modeling40. In parallel, patient advocacy organizations, including the White Sutton Syndrome Foundation and the Cure POGZ Disorders Foundation, continue to sponsor translational grant initiatives and coordinate patient-focused registries across North America and Europe19.

## **Conclusions and Strategic Outlook**

Research into White-Sutton syndrome has transitioned from initial clinical delineation toward detailed mechanistic investigations of chromatin biology and targeted therapeutic repositioning7. High-resolution epigenetic mapping has clarified POGZ's role in maintaining topologically associating domain architecture, balancing euchromatin transcription alongside the G9a/GLP methyltransferase and ADNP complexes, and directing intermediate progenitor dynamics during corticogenesis8.  
Preclinical work demonstrating that *Pogz* deficiency produces a cell-autonomous state of cortical glutamatergic hyperexcitability has supported clinical translation, leading to an active multicenter European Phase II trial evaluating the AMPA receptor antagonist perampanel9. Concurrently, translational discoveries surrounding the oxytocin receptor promoter, cannabinoid sensitivity in drug-resistant epilepsy, and AAV-mediated gene replacement roadmaps provide a diverse preclinical therapeutic pipeline27.  
Pending the validation and regulatory approval of disease-modifying therapies, the clinical management of White-Sutton syndrome centers on early genetic confirmation, targeted screening for structural brain, cardiac, and renal anomalies, structured behavioral interventions, and proactive management of cyclic vomiting, chronic constipation, and adult obesity4. Multidisciplinary rehabilitation—combining early motor speech therapy for Childhood Apraxia of Speech, augmentative communication integration, core physical stabilization, and sensory therapies—remains the standard of care to maximize functional independence and long-term quality of life4.
